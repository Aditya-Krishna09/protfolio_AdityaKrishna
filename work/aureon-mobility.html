01 / OVERVIEW

The context behind the work.

An international expansion strategy case for AUREON Mobility, a fictional multinational automotive company. The work compared expansion choices in India, Mexico, and Poland while balancing demand growth, plant capacity, cross-border transfers, supplier resilience, and capital discipline.

The case company’s demand baseline was 640K units in 2026, with 2030 demand of 760K. Practical capacity was modelled at 711K, creating a 49K-unit shortfall. The analysis compared how expansion in India, Mexico, or Poland would serve regional demand and affect freight, investment, and operational risk.

My role: Individual participation.

02 / THE CHALLENGE

Start with the real problem.

The company needed to add capacity while managing uneven regional demand, plant utilisation, transfer costs, supplier concentration, and uncertainty around the strength and geography of future demand.

03 / APPROACH

How the work came together.

01

Map the demand gap

Separate regional volume growth from contribution economics and compare demand with practical capacity.

02

Compare footprints

Evaluate India, Mexico, and Poland on capital per added unit, transfer requirements, and freight.

03

Stress-test scenarios

Test base, downside, Asia-resilient, and upside demand patterns before committing capital.

04

Stage investment

Propose demand-gated India expansion, supplier-resilience work, and retained capital pending diligence.

OUTCOME / STATUS

Completed a Round 1 case submission. The case company is fictional; forecasts, investment options, and unit economics are case calculations and analyst assumptions—not actual company performance or approved investment.

Contribution after freight is not profit, EBITDA, or NPV. Plant operating costs, tax, depreciation, working capital, and full project DCF were identified as limitations.
